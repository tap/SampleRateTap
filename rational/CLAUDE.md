# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this
engine. The family's rules — the dependency rule, never routing by rate, the substrate
discipline, style, build and test — are in the root [`CLAUDE.md`](../CLAUDE.md); this file is
the engine's charter, which is what makes it different from its siblings.

## What this is

**`rational`** (`tap::sr::rational`) — synchronous small-factor L/M sample rate conversion
*within* a rate family, as fast as possible: ratios L/M with L, M ∈ {2^a · 3^b}, gcd(L, M) = 1,
realised as chains of Nyquist (L-th-band) stages and, beside them, recursive (IIR) half-band
stages by 2 and their cascades (R21–R25, the family's D20) and a 1:1 guard band filter
(R26–R28). Header-only C++20 under `include/tap/sr/rational/`, built on the shared substrate from DspTap (`tap::dsp`: the L-th-band
designer `nyquist.h` and the stage composition `chain.h` landed there first, with the
sample-format traits, dot kernels and row-sum quantization). **`PLAN.md` is the authoritative
roadmap** — charter, the settled decisions R1–R28, the generated 14 × 14 coverage matrix,
layout, test strategy, milestones M0–M6 and acceptance criteria. Read it before implementing
anything; do not re-derive what it settles (the compile-time ratio, one Nyquist filter per stage,
stage factoring by MACs then by stage count, `bridge` at the lowest k, the profile vocabulary,
latency as an exact rational).

## The charter constraints (load-bearing)

- **Within a family only.** The 48 kHz family (8 … 384 kHz) and the 44.1 kHz family
  (11.025 … 176.4 kHz). Crossing them is `bridge` (147/160), reached by a chain the caller
  writes; absorbing a clock is `async`, by composition. The charter is a `static_assert` on
  `ratio<L, M>`: L and M of the form 2^a · 3^b, lowest terms, L ≠ M. Nothing else compiles.
  A 1:1 guard band filter is admitted beside the ratios (R26): it changes no rate, so the
  charter's `static_assert` stays as it is.
- **Never routed to by rate (D12).** `ratio<L, M>` names the number, a chain names the stages;
  there is no `(in_hz, out_hz)` lookup here or in the C ABI (whose constants name chains). The
  coverage matrix in PLAN.md documents chains; it dispatches nothing.
- **Speed-first, like `bridge`.** The ratio is a compile-time type, so every trip count and
  schedule is a compile-time fact; the stage factoring is chosen by MACs on the measured lengths
  (R3: since X-a3 the 4th-band stage is one of the bands it chooses from, decision 6 as
  amended, and it wins at the high-rate end of every chain by 8 and above, while ↑4 itself stays
  two half-bands, cheaper by MACs; on v0.7's pins a relaxed half-band stayed at m = 7, which X17
  traced to the designer's β, not a floor of the Kaiser fit, PLAN.md 2.1 and the S1 ledger),
  once per attenuation tier since X-a4 (at `economy` for the chain a row runs at the 70 dB
  profiles, at `transparent` for the 120 dB tier's; X4); every inaudible quality-vs-speed trade goes to speed in the
  default `economy` profile. A chain's stages are each designed at their own rate: the design
  divisor (PLAN.md 3.1), computed by `chain.h` at compile time, with pinned lengths per divisor.
  Change the matrix only through the generator, then regenerate the rows header and the plan's
  tables.
- **One Nyquist filter per FIR stage (R2, R4).** Length N = 2mL − 1, centre tap exactly 1, every
  L-th tap from it exactly 0, every polyphase branch at DC gain 1 — `tap::dsp::nyquist.h`'s
  contract, pinned by `test_design.cpp` through this engine's build. The up and down designs of
  one band are the same prototype; a mixed ratio's band is its larger factor.
- **Recursive stages are a stage type of their own (R21, D20), never a profile.**
  `basic_iir_stage<S, Ch, R, P>` over `iir_stage_base<S, R, P>` (K12): R `up2` / `down2` only
  (by 3 is planned, by 4 measured and not shipped, both `static_assert`s that say so), `P` an
  `iir_phase` (`nonlinear`, `near_linear`), the four profiles at the FIR stages' numbers with
  compile-time or committed designs at the divisors 1, 2, 4 and 8 (R22). The machine is the
  engine's (the commutator and its latch, the near-linear delay ring, the accounting, flush, A12's
  out-of-line blocks against a cursor); the designs and step kernels are DspTap's (`iir_design.h`,
  `iir_kernels.h`), never forked here. Its latency is nominal, never `latency_output_frames()`
  (R23); its flush ends in an exactly zero state under the host's flush-to-zero, which no stage
  ever sets (R24, I9); its Q15 / Q31 outputs are bit-pinned and its float ones held to the
  direct-form golden leg by tolerance (R25). Every recursive test runs under the host's
  flush-to-zero (`tests/support/flush_to_zero.h`), and the no-flush honest limit runs in an image
  of its own (`TAP_DSP_ALLOW_SUBNORMALS` is one setting per image).
- **The guard band filter is a 1:1 stage of its own (R26–R28), never a ratio or an option on a
  stage.** `guard_band_filter<S, Ch, P>` over `guard_band_filter_base<S, P>` (K12), `P` a
  `guard_phase` (`nonlinear`: the odd-order Chebyshev II over DspTap's `biquad_cascade`, its
  latency nominal; `linear`: the even-length symmetric FIR over `dot_row`, its latency exact,
  (N − 1) / 2 frames), `guard_band_settings` the profiles' numbers at the orders 5 / 7 / 7 / 7
  (the IIR plan's question 14) plus a custom form designed at run time. The designs are
  DspTap's (`k_guard_band_cheby2`, `k_guard_band_fir`, `design_cheby2_lowpass`,
  `design_guard_band_linear`), never forked here. A mixed chain is the caller's
  `tap::dsp::chain` (R27): no named IIR chains, no coverage-matrix rows. The floating Chebyshev
  II at orders 7 and above, like the near-linear `transparent` stage at divisor 1, may settle in
  a zero-input limit cycle under flush-to-zero, an honest limit pinned below 16 (and 8) times
  the smallest normal (R28); everything else flushes exactly.
- **Two machines for the FIR stage, chosen by the ratio at compile time.** An interpolator or a mixed ratio runs
  bridge's L-phase schedule machine with each phase row trimmed to its nonzero span (an
  interpolator's centre phase is one tap, a copy); a decimator runs the M-branch commutator,
  its nonzero branches summed under one finalize (`tap::dsp::accumulate_row`). MACs per output
  are the design's nonzero counts; the structural zeros are never multiplied (R4).
- **The channel count is the type's (A13, `docs/CHANNELS_PLAN.md`).** `Ch`, a compile-time
  constant of at least 1, is the second template parameter of `basic_stage` and `basic_chain`
  and the first of the aliases; zero fails to compile
  (`rational.Stage.ZeroChannelsFailToCompileWithTheMessage`). The count-independent state and
  cold code are `stage_base<S, R>`'s, built once per format and ratio (K12): only `process`,
  `pull` and `flush` are compiled per count. The C ABI keeps a run-time count, one and two
  channels native and any other a fan-out of one-channel converters, pinned bit for bit
  (`CApi.FanOutIsTheNativeConverterBitForBit`). Gate 0 found no placement of the per-count block
  that kept every row on both Arm targets; the maintainer accepted the out-of-line one with
  three rows above the rule, and at A13b one row gated at the midpoint of its two builds
  (PLAN.md section 6, the A13 record).
- **The hot path belongs to the engine, not its caller (A12).** `process`, `pull` and `flush`
  are out of line (`TAP_SR_RATIONAL_NOINLINE`), each the block for its channel count itself, and
  each runs against a `cursor` (the stream state and the hot constants as locals, `open()` /
  `close()`). Inside the block loops a
  member read is a reload per frame — an out-of-line `this` is reachable from `compact()`'s
  `memmove` — so the state and the decimator's row spans travel in the cursor; the
  interpolator's spans, indexed by a runtime phase, stay member reads because that measured
  cheaper (PLAN.md section 6, the A12 lever). The instruction counts with and without
  exceptions are within 0.1 % of each other on every row but one (A13b): the M55's
  `ratio23_q15_eco`, 5 % apart because GCC allocates the block's registers differently in the
  two builds (their GIMPLE identical; PLAN.md section 6, the A13 record). The ratchet runs both
  configurations against one set of baselines, so that row's baseline is the midpoint of its
  two builds: re-record it there, never with `--update` from one of them.
- **Correctness before optimization.** Scipy golden vectors, exhaustive phase sweeps,
  `decimate.h` as a second golden at ↓2 / ↓3 / ↓6, and the 182-row coverage matrix test gate
  every optimization that follows (PLAN.md section 5). Regenerate the vectors only when the
  design or the table's gain rule changes, with the generator, and commit the header.

## Profiles

`bridge`'s four names — `super_economy`, `economy` (default), `balanced`, `transparent` — as
(stopband A, passband edge as a fraction of the chain's lowest rate): 70 dB at 1/3, 3/8, 19/48
and 120 dB at 5/12, which are `bridge`'s 16 / 18 / 19 / 20 kHz at 48 kHz. Each carries the
pinned taps per branch m for the bands 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 (N = 2mB − 1; `design.h`'s table): a pin
is the smallest m whose design, its Kaiser β taken for A + 4 dB (X17, `k_design_beta_offset_db`),
meets the stopband A with ≥ 1 dB margin on the 16384-point design grid, and
the grid is part of the pin — the designer's 1024-point default under-pins two rows. Tests are typed over
`float` / `int16_t` / `int32_t` from M3 with `double` as the oracle; measured numbers are stated
in comments with their provenance.

```sh
# from the repository root (this engine lives in rational/; the root builds every engine)
cmake -S . -B build -DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release -DTAP_SR_RATIONAL_WERROR=ON
cmake --build build
ctest --test-dir build --output-on-failure -L '^rational$'
```

`rational.Ratio.ChartersFailToCompileWithTheMessage` configures `tests/compile_fail/`, a
`try_compile` project, with this build's compiler or toolchain file: the four rejected ratios, one
per static_assert of the charter, must fail to compile *with* the charter's message.
