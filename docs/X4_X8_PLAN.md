## 1. The ask

The maintainer asked for two things:

1. **Support for ×4, ×8 and ×16 down and up stages** (×16 added in v3). The example use is a
   4096-point FFT with finer resolution in the low bands, which means decimating, processing
   and interpolating.
2. **Aliases named for the factor, such as 4× and 8×,** so the code reads by intent while
   running the cheapest chain.

The second asks for something the book currently argues against. Part VII
(`book/src/part7/chain.md:131-136`) says the chains are "named by their stages in order,
because the name *is* the chain and a name that said 'times 8' would be a lookup." A plain
`using down_by_8 = …` is no lookup: the type is fixed once, in the header, and the matrix still
dispatches nothing. So the aliases are compatible with D12, but that sentence and
`rational/PLAN.md` decision 6 change with them (section 4).

## 2. What exists

- **Single stages:**
  - ×8 exists as `up_8` / `down_8`, which are `ratio<8, 1>` / `ratio<1, 8>`, one 8th-band stage
    each (`rational/include/tap/sr/rational/ratio.h:139-153`).
  - There is no `down_4` / `up_4`. Decision 6 and R3 make ×4 two half-bands
    (`rational/PLAN.md:108`, `:1244`).
  - **Measured:** `basic_stage<S, Ch, ratio<1, 4>>` and `<4, 1>` compile and run today. The
    band-4 pins exist at every divisor (`design.h:38-85`), because they serve 4/3 and 3/4.
  - The C ABI's `tap_sr_rational_create_stage` refuses 4/1 and 1/4: `make_single` has no
    case for them (`tap_sr_rational_capi.cpp:369-400`), a test pins the refusal
    (`test_capi.cpp:238`, "(decision 6)"), and the header documents "one of the vocabulary's
    fourteen" (`tap_sr_rational_capi.h:117-118`).
- **The natural names are taken.** `down_8` is the single stage, so the aliases need new names
  (X1).
- **The matrix chains for these ratios** (`chain.h:194-227`):
  - 1/4 is `down_2_down_2` and 4/1 is `up_2_up_2` in both families;
  - the 48 family's 1/8 is `down_3_ratio_3_4_down_2` and its 8/1 is `up_2_ratio_4_3_up_3`;
  - the 44.1 family's 1/8 is `ratio_3_8_down_3` and its 8/1 is `up_3_ratio_8_3`;
  - 1/16 is `down_8_down_2` and 16/1 is `up_2_up_8` in both families.
- **There are 20 named chains** (`chain.h`; `chain.h:190-193`'s comment, "the 48 kHz family's
  twenty, plus the two", double-counts and is corrected in X-a1).
- **×16 has no single stage.** The vocabulary's bands are 2, 3, 4, 6 and 8 (`design.h`), so
  ×16 is always a chain.
- **The C ABI names each chain by its stages**, with stable values, 28 of them (0 to 27, the
  20 multi-stage chains at 8 to 27; `capi/tap_sr_rational_capi.h:48-79`).
  `TAP_SR_RATIONAL_CHAIN_COUNT` (28) is both their count and the exclusive bound
  `create_format` checks (`capi.cpp:441`); `test_capi.cpp:185` pins it and `:234` expects
  `create(CHAIN_COUNT)` to fail; the ctypes binding maps names by their position in a list
  "in value order" (`tap_sr_rational_py.py:30`, `:201`).
- **The ratchet runs no ×8 chain.** Its one chain workload is `down2_down2_q15_eco`, and the
  ceilings measure the `up_2` / `down_2` probes.
- **DspTap** has nothing at these ratios that fits the use. `decimate.h` decimates by 2, 3 or 6
  to 16 kHz only, mono, with absolute-Hz profiles and no interpolator. TapTools depends on
  DspTap only (section 6.1), pinned at 2137d86, a commit from before DspTap v0.1.0.

### 3.2 The chain types carry no rate, so one alias serves both families (**measured**)

- **`chain.h` carries no rate.**
  - `chain_rates` takes every rate relative to the chain's input (`r[0] = 1`, `chain.h:83`).
  - Each stage's design divisor is its lower rate over the chain's lowest (`chain.h:95-102`).
  - The constructor builds stage i at `p.relaxed(k_divisors[I])` (`chain.h:166`).
  - So the chain `↓3 · 3/4 · ↓2` costs 105 at `economy` whichever family runs it (`x4x8/family`).
- **The families differ only through the generator's intermediate lattice.**
  - Intermediates in `matrix.py` are base × 2^a 3^b for base 1000 or 11025, with b from 0 to 3
    (`matrix.py:201-212`).
  - Relative to a 48-family r_min this admits factors of 3^-1. 32 kHz is r_min · 8/3 for
    12 kHz, for example. Relative to a 44.1-family r_min it does not: 29.4 kHz is not on
    the lattice.
  - So 88.2 → 11.025 cannot take ↓3 · 3/4 · ↓2 and pays 124 instead of 105, which `PLAN.md`
    3.4 records accurately.
  - **A side finding:** the comment at `matrix.py:203-206` says the opposite ("the same relative
    lattice in both families, so a ratio's chain is the same chain in either") and should be
    corrected whatever is decided here.
- **↓4 · ↓2 needs no lattice change.** Its one intermediate is twice the output rate: 24, 48 or
  96 kHz in the 48 family, 22.05 or 44.1 kHz in the 44.1 family, all lattice points and
  supported rates. Once the 4th-band stage is admitted, 1/8 and 8/1 become the same chain in
  both families, and one family-free alias is the matrix's chain in both.

The single stages stay the low-latency spelling. At ×8 they cost twice the MACs to save 16
samples (0.33 ms) of round trip against X3's chain; at ×4 they cost 26 % more to save 24
samples (0.5 ms). ×16 has no single stage; ↓4 · ↓4 and ↑4 · ↑4 are its low-latency spelling,

32768 (×8) or 65536 (×16) full-rate samples, so the stages are under 0.7 % of the latency and
MACs should decide. A latency-first caller (a small FFT, or processing at the low rate without
one) spells the single stage.

## 4. Decisions on the aliases

### X0. The spelling (v4, decided: the maintainer's rule)

The maintainer finds today's spelling clumsy (`down_3_ratio_3_4_down_2`) and asks for
`down3_ratio3over4_down2`. The rule:
- **a factor joins its word:** `down3`, `up8` (today `down_3`, `up_8`);
- **a mixed ratio reads as a fraction:** `ratio3over4` (today `ratio_3_4`);
- **an underscore separates the stages of a chain:** `down3_ratio3over4_down2`, `up2_up8`.

(That chain is one X3 retires, so it ships under neither spelling; the rule stands.)

What it renames: `rational`'s vocabulary wherever it is spelled, which reaches one file
outside `rational`.
- the 14 ratio aliases (`ratio.h:139-153`), and `down4` / `up4` with X3;
- the 20 named chains (`chain.h`), with `chain.h:190-193`'s comment corrected;
- the C ABI's constants: `TAP_SR_RATIONAL_DOWN_8_DOWN_2` becomes `TAP_SR_RATIONAL_DOWN8_DOWN2`,
  every value unchanged. The ABI stays the same; C sources change only their spelling. The
  six chains X3 retires (3.3) are not renamed: their old names go straight into
  `retired_words.txt` in X-a2;
- **inside `rational`:** `matrix.py`'s names (and the f-string at `matrix.py:471`) and
  `tests/coverage/matrix_rows.h`; the tests; `rational/bench/` (`icount_main`,
  `callmax_main`, `cmp_main`, `host_main`, `probe_main.cpp` and `compare/bench_compare.cpp`,
  about 34 lines); `rational/examples/multirate_chain.cpp`;
  `tools/reference/make_reference_vectors.py` and the `reference_vectors.h` it generates
  (regenerated, never hand-edited, the PR saying the change is spelling only); the ctypes
  binding and the executed notebooks; the README's quick start;
- **outside it:** `async/examples/pico2_cyccnt/main.cpp:293-295` (`rational::up_2`, `down_2`
  and `ratio_2_3`), which CI's `pico2-build` job compiles twice (`ci.yml:1005-1008`,
  `:1032`); the root `README.md:77` (`rational::converter<2, down_2>`); the book's Part VII
  (`chain.md`, and `charter.md:151-155`); `rational/PLAN.md`'s tables.

Under D7 the old spellings are removed, not aliased, and `scripts/retired_words.txt` gains
them. The gate compiles each pattern case-sensitively, and `_` is a word character, so `\b`
never matches inside an underscore-joined name. The patterns are therefore:
- `(?<![A-Za-z0-9])(up|down)_[2368](?![0-9])` and
  `(?<![A-Za-z0-9])ratio_[0-9]_[0-9](?![0-9])`, which also catch the names inside chains;
- `TAP_SR_RATIONAL_(UP|DOWN|RATIO)_[0-9]` for the C constants, which X0's spellings and the
  `_BY` macros do not match.

What they would otherwise hit is handled in X-a1:
- the charter's compile-fail fixtures (`rational/tests/compile_fail/ratio_5_1.cpp`,
  `ratio_4_2.cpp`, `ratio_2_2.cpp`, `ratio_1_1.cpp`, rejected ratios, not retired names) get
  an `ALLOWED` entry for `ratio_[0-9]_[0-9]\.cpp`;
- the records `docs/CHANNELS_PLAN.md`, `docs/CHANNELS_PLAN_AUDIT.md` and
  `docs/channels/multicount/CMakeLists.txt` join the gate's `HISTORY` list rather than being
  rewritten (the multicount probe keeps compiling against the reference headers);
- the gate runs over the whole tree on X-a1 before the list is committed.

The plans' own records spell the old names by necessity: this plan (`docs/X4_X8_PLAN.md`)
and its audit (`docs/PLANS_AUDIT_2026-11.md`) join `HISTORY` in X-a1. The IIR plan
(`docs/IIR_PLAN.md`) already uses X0's spelling (checked against the patterns above).

**When:** X-a1, the first PR of 0.8.0, so consumers break once (section 8).

**Not renamed:** other engines' names (`converter_to_48k`), and DspTap's
`tap::dsp::decimate_by_2/3/6`, a different repository and contract (decided, question 9).

**The aliases' `_by`** (X1) is the rule's one exception: there an underscore marks a name
chosen by intent, not a stage boundary.

### X1. The names

### 6.1 What it needs beyond the aliases

TapTools' `stft.h` is the scaffold for `nr.h` and `spectra.h`:

- double, mono, Hann window, overlap 4;
- its latency is one frame (`stft.h:97`).

TapTools reaches DspTap only (`.gitmodules`), so `rational`'s stages are out of its reach

| 48 kHz | 4 | 12 kHz | 2.93 Hz | 341 ms | 16384 points |
| 48 kHz | 8 | 6 kHz | 1.46 Hz | 683 ms | 32768 points |
| 44.1 kHz | 8 | 5.5125 kHz | 1.35 Hz | 743 ms | 32768 points |

### 6.3 The structure: process the change, not the band

  out[n] = x[n − T] + up( Δ( down(x) ) )[n],  with T = D · τ_down + D · L_op + τ_up,

where:

- Δ is the op's change (result minus input), confined to the usable bins (X15);
- τ_down is the decimator's latency in low-rate frames;
- L_op is the op's latency in low-rate frames;
- τ_up is the interpolator's latency in full-rate samples.

Every term is exact. **T is an integer for chains of integer-factor stages**, whose delays are
integers at each stage's higher rate: every alias's chain, with X3. It is not for every chain:
the 44.1 family's ×8 chains today (3/8 · ↓3 and ↑3 · 8/3) carry a 3 in their latencies'
denominators (98⅓ full-rate samples each way at `economy`). The scaffold rejects a
non-integer D · τ_down + τ_up at compile time (X10).

- **An identity op gives the input delayed by T, bit for bit, by construction:** Δ is exactly
  zero, and a zero-primed interpolator turns zeros into exact zeros, for any T and any chain.
  It is a structural guarantee, not a measurement of T; only a cancellation pins T (6.5).
- **The aliasing in the untouched bins is never re-injected,** because the full-band path
  carries those bins.
- **No guard band filter is needed,** for the same reason: the change carries nothing in the
  guard band, and the full-band path carries the guard band untouched.
- **Bands compose by addition** (x[n − T] + up₈(Δ₈) + up₄(Δ₄)), with disjoint ownership and
  complementary tapers where bands meet. No crossover filter is designed at any point. Each
  band has its own T (at `economy`, N = 4096: T₄ = 110 + 4 · 4096 = 16494 and
  T₈ = 206 + 8 · 4096 = 32974), so **every band is delayed to the largest T**. Each extra
  delay is an integer and, when it is a multiple of the band's D, is held at the band's low
  rate before its interpolator: the ×4 band's 16480 samples are 4120 frames at 12 kHz.
- **Removing a low-band component (Δ = −X) leaves the round trip's passband ripple:** −60 to
  −92 dB at `economy`, and about −110 dB at worst at `transparent` (−109 dB near the band
  edge on the audit's dense sweep; v5's ≤ −120 dB sampled four frequencies).
- **Cancellation needs linear phase and an exact delay.** A round-trip phase error φ leaves
  2 sin(φ / 2), so −40 dB needs φ ≤ 0.57° and −60 dB needs φ ≤ 0.057°. φ is the sum over every
  stage down and up.
  - The FIR stages meet that by construction, and their round trip is pinned at −60 dB
    (6.5, 6.6).
  - The IIR plan's near-linear variant targets 0.57° over the whole round trip (its question
    5): its round trips are pinned at −40 dB, a lower floor than the FIR's, with each test
    stating its stage kind's threshold. The IIR plan's spike may raise it.
  - Its nonlinear variant suits analysis only (decimate, then FFT); the scaffold refuses it
    (X10).

### 6.4 Decisions for the round trip

- **X9. Formats and channels:** the DspTap scaffold is typed over float and double, its
  sample type from its stages, with cross-precision agreement pinned, as DspTap's discipline
  requires of every primitive. TapTools uses double first (`stft.h` is double), float
  later. `Ch` comes from the stages, and the first consumer is mono.
- **The bins a process may touch (decided, v5):** the `economy` passband, 75 % of the low
  band (bins up to 3/8 of the low rate; 1.125 kHz at 3 kHz), as 6.2 measures.
- **X10. Where the scaffold lives.** *Decided (v5):* DspTap, as a primitive generic over
  `sync_stage` (placeholder `subband_delta<Down, Up, Op>`). It holds the delay line, T from
  the stages, and the low-rate op. Its battery uses test-local stages on `nyquist.h`, as
  `chain.h` was proven on `decimate.h`. `stft.h` gains a change mode that does the masking
  and tapering. *Declined:* all of it in TapTools.
  - **Where T comes from (v6):** each stage reports an exact latency, or an exact delay path

    non-integer T at compile time, and refuses a stage with neither, such as the nonlinear
    IIR variant. The concept is defined in DspTap 0.3.0, so admitting the IIR stages later
    needs no DspTap change.
  - **DspTap's checklist for a new primitive:** a README section and the primitive count in

  plan's matrix and spelling. **In DspTap, one 0.3.0** carries the scaffold (X-c), the IIR

  plan's IIR-b both start at the tag, so each waits for the later of the two (accepted, v6).
  The IIR stages plug into the scaffold through X10's delay-path concept, under 6.3's phase
  condition.
- **X14. The first consumer (decided, v5): a new TapTools object** for precise low-band EQ
  and notching: narrow cuts and boosts at the low band's resolution (0.73 Hz at ×16), for hum
  harmonics and room modes. It is 6.6's tone removal as a product. Its design is a TapTools

  only (decimate, then FFT, no way back) is not a goal now.
- **D19. Rates are named by ratio (decided, v6, the maintainer's A).** The root `CLAUDE.md`
  says no rate is served by an engine whose charter does not name it, and the family's rates
  are 14, the lowest 8 kHz (`PLAN.md` 2.1); `rational/PLAN.md` makes 7.35 kHz a non-goal
  because "adding a rate is a family-plan change". This plan's example, tests and TapTools
  scenarios run at 6, 5.5125, 3 and 2.75625 kHz. D19 restates the rule: `rational`'s charter
  names ratios inside a family (L, M ∈ 2^a · 3^b, the rate lattice base × 2^a 3^b, not a list
  of rates), so any rate a family rate reaches by such a ratio is in its charter; the 14
  rates are the coverage matrix's pinned set, not a limit. The rule's purpose stands: the
  1000/1001 pull-down rates are no 2^a 3^b ratio of a family rate, so they stay outside
  `rational`'s charter, and they are still never `async`'s. X-b records D19 in the family plan and amends the root

- **X15. The usable band: precondition or enforcement (new, v6).** 6.3's two claims (no
  aliasing re-injected; processed bins alias-free) hold only while Δ is confined to bins up
  to p · r_low. X10 puts the masking in TapTools' `stft.h`, not in the scaffold, so an op that
  does not go through `stft.h` (a time-domain op, or 6.5's pitch-dependent process) has no
  mask, and its Δ covers the transition band, which `up()` re-injects. *Recommended:* the
  scaffold states the confinement as a precondition on `Op` in its contract, and a DspTap
  test pins what an unmasked op that touches the transition band produces, as an honest
  limit. *Alternative:* the scaffold enforces it with a low-rate filter on Δ, at its cost and
  delay.

### 6.5 Tests for the round trip

- **DspTap** (typed over float and double, with cross-precision agreement):
  - `Subband.IdentityOpIsTheInputDelayedBitForBit` (structural, 6.3);
  - `Subband.RemovingTheBandReachesTheStagesRippleFloor`: an in-band tone removed through the
    test-local `nyquist.h` stages, the residual pinned at those stages' floor, with T ± 1
    required to fail. This is the test that pins T against the signal path (the audit: with
    T + 1 the identity test still passes, and the removal fails, −31.46 dB against −64.05);
  - `Subband.LatencyIsTheExactSumOfItsParts`;
  - `Subband.ChunkingIsBitIdentical`;
  - `Subband.FlushDrainsBothPaths`;
  - `Subband.BandsComposeByAddition`, with bands whose T differ, so the alignment is tested;
  - `Subband.UnmaskedChangeIsReinjected` (X15's honest limit, if the precondition is chosen);
  - `Subband.NonIntegerDelayIsRejected` (a compile-fail case);
  - on every QEMU leg the battery fits, or excluded by name with the reason.
- **TapTools `SCENARIO`s**, named as sentences, at 48 → 12, 48 → 6, 48 → 3, 44.1 → 11.025,
  44.1 → 5.5125 and 96 → 12 (in the charter under D19):
  - "processed bins are alias-free to the profile";
  - "a modification's images stay below the stopband";
  - "full cancellation reaches the ripple floor", pinned per profile from a dense sweep

  - a `tap::dsp::yin`-oracle check of a pitch-dependent low-band process.

### 6.6 A demonstration: 48 kHz and ÷16 (v4, **measured**)

The maintainer asked for a program that shows the use case. A scratch version runs today
against the shipping headers (`examples/low_band_fft_48k_div16.cpp` in the session
scratchpad, committed by X-0; GCC 13, x86-64, double, mono).
- **The signal:** 12 s at 48 kHz: 200 Hz and 205 Hz at −12 dBFS each, 5 Hz apart, and
  5 kHz at −20 dBFS, above the low band.
- **The chains:** the matrix's 1/16 and 16/1, today's `down_8_down_2` and `up_2_up_8`
  (`down_by16` / `up_by16` under X0 and X1), at `economy`. 48 kHz → 3 kHz.
- **The process:** a 4096-point STFT at 3 kHz (Hann, overlap 4) that removes the 205 Hz tone
  by zeroing the 9 bins around it, through 6.3's structure: the change alone (minus those
  bins), interpolated back and added to the input delayed by T.

What it shows:

| | |
|---|---|
| 4096 points at 48 kHz (11.7 Hz bins) | **measured:** one peak; 200 and 205 Hz are 0.43 bins apart |
| 4096 points at 3 kHz after ÷16 (0.73 Hz bins) | **measured:** two peaks, 45 dB of dip between them |
| T, the round trip's delay | 16 × 12.9375 + 207 = 414 samples exactly (8.62 ms) |
| stages' cost | **measured:** 20.62 MACs per full-rate sample |
| an identity process | the input delayed by T, bit for bit, by construction (6.3) |
| removing 205 Hz | **measured:** 205 Hz −64.05 dB; 200 Hz and 5 kHz unchanged (0.00 dB); with T + 1, only −31.46 dB |

*Recommended:* it ships as `rational/examples/low_band_fft.cpp` in X-b, written with X1's
aliases, built by every CI job that builds the examples, with a section in
`rational/README.md`. Its claims are pinned by tests in `rational/tests/`:
- `rational.LowBand.IdentityChangeIsTheInputDelayedBitForBit`, at ×4, ×8 and ×16
  (structural);
- `rational.LowBand.RemovalNeedsTheExactDelay`: the removal at ×4, ×8 and ×16 reaches
  60 dB with T, and fails with T ± 1;
- `rational.LowBand.ResolvesTonesFiveHertzApartAfterDown16`: two peaks with at least 40 dB
  between them;
- `rational.LowBand.RemovingOneToneLeavesTheOthers`: the removed tone at least 60 dB down,
  the others within 0.01 dB.

**The tests run in blocks, within about 1 MB of state**, not on the demo's 12 s buffers
(4.6 MB each): the M55 image has 2 MB for data, bss and heap and the M33 4 MB, and one
4096-point frame at 3 kHz is already 512 KiB per double buffer at the full rate. The ×16
double cases, which cannot fit (and run soft-float on the M33), are excluded by name in
`bare_metal_main.cpp` with that reason and run hosted; the ×4 and ×8 cases run on every leg.

The example runs offline, on whole buffers, so the structure is plain to read. A streaming
version comes with the scaffold (X10), which replaces its hand-written delay and sum.

## 7. Honest limits

- **Resolution is bought with time.** 1.46 Hz bins are 0.7 s frames, and ×16's 0.73 Hz bins
  are 1.4 s frames. A change to the low band spreads over that frame, and pre-echo of a
  modification reaches back up to one frame.
- **Not every low-rate bin can be processed:** a quarter of them at `economy`, 17 % at
  `transparent`.
- **Each alias is the cheapest chain at its own tier** (within R3's 10 % at ×16): `down_by8`
  at the 70 dB profiles, `down_by8_transparent` at 120 dB. Either one run at the other
  tier costs more, by up to 43 % at ×16 (X4); the docstrings say so.
- **48 → 6, 48 → 3, 44.1 → 5.5125 and 44.1 → 2.75625 kHz are in the charter** under D19,
  since the designs are relative, but no matrix row pins them. The round trip's tests pin its
  own pairs.
- **A non-integer T is refused** (6.3), so a chain with a factor of 3 in its latency's
  denominator cannot enter the scaffold.
- **An IIR round trip cancels to −40 dB, not the FIR's −60** (6.3).
- **`down8` and `down_by8` are one word apart** and mean different things (X1).
